Total Annihilation is a 1997 real-time strategy game about a war between two machine factions: ARM and CORE. You begin with a Commander, build a base, and turn metal and energy into an army.

Land, sea & airTanks, walking robots, aircraft and fleets fight across terrain that shapes the battle.

The modern GPU renderer

Built on the original.
Rendered for today.

Nanolathe is an independent, open-source 2.5D RTS engine in Go, rebuilding Total Annihilation from an understanding of its original behavior. The modern renderer gives that world room to breathe: closer views, clearer armies, light that reaches nearby units, and water that moves.

These are presentation improvements. They do not change weapon damage, resource costs or the simulation’s rules. The classic rendering path remains available.

Actual engine captures using original game data. Staged scenes are identified. Examples compare Nanolathe’s classic CPU and modern GPU renderers, or toggle the named effect. They are not captures of the retail executable. The interactive material study renders live in your browser.

Beyond the pixels

A better view. A familiar game.

Smoother movement

Modern presentation interpolates between committed game states at the display’s refresh rate. Units can move through half-pixel positions with supersampling, while the simulation still advances at 30 ticks per second.

True-color composition

Original palette artwork feeds a true-color composite. Coverage, light and glow can blend with the scene without forcing the final image back into a palette lookup.

More useful tactical information

Hold Alt/Option to inspect tactical range guides, including weapon and sensor ranges where applicable. The wider view helps you read the battlefield while you plan your next move.

Experimental presentation, under active development. Nanolathe’s current gameplay focus is single-player skirmish and campaigns; multiplayer is outside the current scope.
